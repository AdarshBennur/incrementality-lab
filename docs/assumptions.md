# Core Assumptions

The analysis generated by this platform relies on a number of fundamental assumptions regarding experimental design and statistical validity.

## 1. Random Assignment

We assume that the original A/B test (represented in `marketing_AB.csv`) successfully randomized the subjects. The `user id` field represents distinct subjects, and there was no cross-contamination (e.g., users being assigned to both Treatment and Control simultaneously).

## 2. Independence of Observations

The Two-Proportion Z-Test assumes that each user's conversion outcome is independent of every other user. We assume there are no network effects (e.g., one user converting does not increase the probability of their friend converting).

## 3. Sufficient Sample Size (Central Limit Theorem)

The Z-test approximations (and normal distributions) require a sufficient number of successes and failures. The general rule of thumb is at least 10 conversions and 10 non-conversions per group.

- The global test easily satisfies this.
- _Caveat_: When drilling down into extremely narrow sub-segments (e.g., "Tablet" users in "APAC"), the sample size may drop below reliable thresholds, inflating the variance of the Confidence Interval.

## 4. Constant Baseline Revenue

When calculating Incremental Revenue, we assume the average order value (AOV) generated by the Control group accurately reflects what the Treatment group would have generated without intervention.

- We use a blended global average order value to estimate baseline revenue to smooth out extreme outliers in synthetic revenue generation.

## 5. Synthetic Enrichment Caveats

Because business dimensions (Geography, Device, Audience Segment) were synthetically generated (albeit deterministically based on user ID), any _correlation_ between these segments and the conversion rate is entirely due to the original dataset's underlying variance interacting with the pseudo-random hashing.

- As such, "Insights" drawn from segment-level analysis in this specific lab environment should be viewed as demonstrations of the _capability_ of the platform, not genuine market insights.
